Biography

I am a PhD student in the Sferruzzi-Perri Lab1 in the Department of Physiology, Development & Neuroscience at the University of Cambridge, and a member of Cambridge Reproduction and Hughes Hall. My project asks which mechanistic pathways are altered in normal and obese pregnancies. To answer it, I combine metabolomics2 with gene and protein analysis in mouse models.

Before coming to Cambridge, I spent three years as a research assistant in David Wishart’s group at the University of Alberta. There I worked on quantitative metabolomics, on how blood-collection methods change what we measure,3 and on the DrugBank and PathBank databases.4 I have also reviewed what is known about homoarginine, an arginine metabolite that rises through pregnancy.5

I took my undergraduate degree in Honours Physiology at Alberta, where I also contributed to research on cell death in placental trophoblasts,6 the cells that form the interface between mother and fetus.

— A. Z.

Research interests

in four abstracts
Current · 2024–

Maternal obesity and placental metabolism

Which metabolic and signalling pathways shift in obese pregnancies compared with normal ones, mapped by pairing metabolomics with gene and protein analysis in mouse models.

keywords metabolomics; maternal obesity; placenta; mouse models

PhD project, in progress
Review · 2025

Homoarginine

An arginine metabolite whose levels climb through pregnancy and may flag complications such as pre-eclampsia. My review sets out what is known about how it works, beyond its clinical associations.

keywords homoarginine; AGAT; nitric oxide; pre-eclampsia

see ref. 2
Methods · 2025

Blood metabolomes across sampling methods

Comparing 142 metabolites in whole blood, plasma and serum showed that inexpensive microsampling matches a venous draw when the same biofluid is compared.

keywords LC-MS; microsampling; biofluids

see ref. 1
Cell biology · 2023

Trophoblast pyroptosis

Loss of cell-polarity regulators sets off gasdermin-E-mediated pyroptosis, an inflammatory cell death, in the syncytiotrophoblast of the human placenta.

keywords trophoblast; gasdermin E; cell polarity

see ref. 6
